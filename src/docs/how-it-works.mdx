A customer approves a plan once. After that, Mesub pulls the plan's price from
their wallet to yours at every period, and your server asks Mesub whether a
given customer has access.

## The flow

<Sequence />

Your pages never call Mesub and never hold a key. The wallet signs in the
browser, and everything else goes through your server.

## What runs where

| Where       | What it does                                              | What it holds             |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Your pages  | Ask the wallet for its two signatures                     | Nothing secret            |
| Your server | Creates subscriptions, checks access, receives webhooks   | The API key               |
| Mesub       | Sends each charge, retries a missed one, records attempts | No funds                  |
| Solana      | Holds the plan's terms and moves the tokens               | The customer's delegation |

Your customer has no Mesub account. Mesub finds them by the id you give them
(`external_id`), by the wallet that pays, or by their email.

## On chain

Mesub does not hold or route the money. Plans and subscriptions are accounts of
the [subscriptions program](https://github.com/solana-foundation/subscriptions)
published by the Solana Foundation, open source
([MIT](https://github.com/solana-foundation/subscriptions/blob/main/LICENSE))
and [audited by Cantina](https://github.com/solana-foundation/subscriptions/blob/main/audits/AUDIT_STATUS.md).
Its address is `De1egAFMkMWZSN5rYXRj9CAdheBamobVNubTsi9avR44`.

Three accounts are involved, all PDAs of that program:

| Account                  | One per                          | What it holds                         |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| `Plan`                   | plan, created by you             | The price, the period, who receives   |
| `SubscriptionAuthority`  | wallet and token, the customer's | The right to move that token          |
| `SubscriptionDelegation` | wallet and plan, the customer's  | The terms signed and the period state |

### The plan

Creating a plan in the [dashboard](https://mesub.io/dashboard#plans) sends one transaction, signed by your wallet,
that creates the `Plan` account with its `mint`, `amount`, `period_hours` and
`end_ts`. `amount` and `period_hours` are immutable.
`destinations` lists the wallets that may receive the money, and `pullers`
lists who may trigger a charge besides you: Mesub's puller is one of them,
which is what lets Mesub charge on schedule without your signature. If you take it off
the plan, charges are blocked until it is back, and your subscribers are not
held to be late for it.

### An end date

A plan runs until you close it, unless you give it an end date when you create
it: the last day it charges, in UTC. It is written on chain as `end_ts`.

| Rule            | What it is                                     |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| At the earliest | One billing period from now, plus five minutes |
| At the latest   | 100 years from now                             |
| Once created    | The date cannot be removed or pushed later     |

A date too close is refused with `end_date_too_soon`, and one too far with
`end_date_too_far`. A plan left unsigned until its date is too close is
refused with `end_date_stale`: change the date, then sign.

For your subscribers, the end date is where everything stops:

- Nobody has access past it, whatever period they paid for.
- The last period is charged in full, even when the plan ends inside it.
- Nothing is charged after it, and every subscription still running ends with
  `plan_ended`.
- Nobody can subscribe from then on.

The terms a customer signs name the date and say the first two. See
[when the plan has an end date](./lifecycle.mdx#when-the-plan-has-an-end-date).

### The subscription

Subscribing creates the customer's `SubscriptionAuthority` for that token if
they have none, and a `SubscriptionDelegation` to your plan, which tracks
`amount_pulled_in_period`, `current_period_start_ts` and `expires_at_ts`. The authority is
approved as delegate on their token account; it can only move tokens when a
delegation says so, and a delegation only allows this:

- up to the plan's `amount` per period, counted in `amount_pulled_in_period`,
- to one of the plan's `destinations`,
- triggered by the plan's owner or one of its `pullers`,
- while `expires_at_ts` is zero or in the future.

The delegation copies the plan's terms when it is created, so a customer keeps
the price they signed for.

Both accounts cost rent, about 0.0036 SOL together. The customer pays it and
gets it back when the subscription is closed.

### A charge

Each period, Mesub's puller sends a transfer instruction. The program checks
the four conditions above, moves the tokens from the customer's token account
to your destination, and updates the delegation's period counters. If the
wallet is short, nothing moves and Mesub retries according to the plan's retry
policy.

### Cancelling

Cancelling sets `expires_at_ts` to the end of the period already paid: access
runs until then, and no further charge can land. The customer signs it, through
your site or directly against the program. Resuming before that date clears it.
Closing the delegation afterwards returns its rent.

### Why your server asks Mesub and not the chain

A delegation that reads active on chain is not proof that the next charge will
land: the wallet may be empty, or the customer may have revoked the token
approval outside the program. Mesub tracks every attempt and its outcome, and
`hasAccess` answers from that record, not from the account alone.
