A subscription has one `status` at a time. It is what `GET /v1/access` and
every webhook carry.

<Lifecycle />

## Statuses

| `status`     | `access`            | `payment_status` | What it means                                                              |
| ------------ | ------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `none`       | false               | `none`           | This customer never subscribed to this plan. Not an error                  |
| `pending`    | false               | `none`           | Reserved, waiting for the wallet's transaction to land                     |
| `active`     | true                | `paid`           | Paid up. The next charge is at `next_charge_at`                            |
| `unpaid`     | true, false on Free | `late`           | A charge missed. A retry is coming                                         |
| `cancelled`  | true until the end  | `paid`           | The customer cancelled. The period already paid runs to `access_until`     |
| `stopped`    | false               | `none`           | The retries ran out. Nothing more is charged                               |
| `ended`      | false               | `none`           | Over. `end_reason` says why                                                |
| `failed`     | false               | `none`           | What landed on chain is not what Mesub reserved, so it is not billed. Rare |
| `superseded` | false               | `none`           | Replaced by a newer subscription of the same wallet to the same plan       |

`ended`, `failed` and `superseded` are final. `stopped` is not: Mesub charges
nothing more, but the wallet's approval stays open on Solana until the customer
cancels then closes it, and until then they can [come back](#coming-back).

Gate on `access`, not on `status`: a `cancelled` subscription still grants
access for a while, and an `unpaid` one may or may not.

A checkout nobody signed turns `expired`. It never appears in `/v1/access`, only
when you read a subscription by its id, and in the `subscription.expired`
webhook.

## Late payments

When a charge misses, most often because the wallet holds less than the price,
the subscription turns `unpaid` and `payment_status` reads `late`.

`late_reason` says why, as the last failed charge found it. It is null on
every other status, and when Mesub cannot name the cause.

| `late_reason`          | What happened                                 | What fixes it    |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| `insufficient_balance` | The wallet holds less than the price          | Adding funds     |
| `approval_revoked`     | The wallet no longer lets Mesub charge it     | Not adding funds |
| `authority_closed`     | The wallet closed its authorisation, for good | Nothing          |

An approval is revoked in the wallet itself, or replaced when another app is
approved on the same token. Read `late_reason` before you tell a customer to
top up.

### On Dev and Business

Mesub retries on its own. A plan gets **3 retries** by default, so a period has
4 tries in all. Each retry waits a quarter of the period, one day at most:

| Plan period | Wait between tries |
| ----------- | ------------------ |
| 1 month     | 1 day              |
| 1 week      | 1 day              |
| 1 day       | 6 hours            |
| 1 hour      | 15 minutes         |

Access stays on while retries run. `next_retry_at` says when the next one is,
`next_retry_number` and `retries_allowed` say which one it is, and
`access_until` is the date of the last retry: if that one fails too, the
subscription turns `stopped` and access ends.

A retry that pays turns it back to `active`. On `insufficient_balance` the
customer has nothing to sign: they only need to add funds.

You can change the number of retries and the wait between them per plan, in
the plan's **Retry policy** in the dashboard. All the retries must fit inside
one period.

### On Free

There are no automatic retries, and an `unpaid` subscription grants **no
access**. You can retry by hand from the dashboard, up to 3 times, until
`retry_deadline`. Past that date the subscription turns `stopped`.

## Cancelling and resuming

The customer cancels by signing one transaction with the wallet that pays. It
moves no money: it records on chain that the subscription ends with the period
already paid.

| Field            | Before   | After cancelling          |
| ---------------- | -------- | ------------------------- |
| `status`         | `active` | `cancelled`               |
| `access`         | true     | true until `access_until` |
| `cancelled_at`   | null     | When they cancelled       |
| `next_charge_at` | a date   | null                      |

Until `access_until`, they can **resume**: one more signature, and the
subscription is `active` again with its next charge back on schedule.

After `access_until`, it turns `ended` with `end_reason: "cancelled"`. The
customer can then **close** it to get the rent of its account back.

## Why a subscription ends

An `ended` subscription carries one `end_reason`:

| `end_reason`       | What happened                                      |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| `cancelled`        | The customer cancelled and the paid period ran out |
| `plan_ended`       | The plan reached its own end date                  |
| `plan_removed`     | You deleted the plan                               |
| `plan_replaced`    | Another plan now stands at its address             |
| `authority_closed` | The wallet closed its authorisation outside Mesub  |
| `closed`           | The customer closed the subscription through Mesub |

It is null on every other status.

## Parked seats

A project holds a limited number of subscribers on Free and Dev. After a
downgrade, the seats over the limit are parked: `paused` is true, the `status`
stays as it was, nothing is charged, and access runs to the end of the period
already paid. Moving back up a tier puts them back on schedule.

## Coming back

A customer whose subscription stopped or ended can subscribe again to the same
plan, with the same wallet. The new subscription takes over, and the old one
turns `superseded`. A stopped subscription holds no seat, so coming back needs
a free one, like any new subscriber: on a full project it is refused with
`seat_cap_reached`.

A return asked within about two minutes of the end of the old subscription's
billing period is refused with `comeback_period_rolling`, so the terms the
wallet signs name the period that is really charged. Wait for the `Retry-After`
it carries, then create the subscription again: a transaction built before the
wait is not sent.

`subscribed_since` is the start of the current one, and
`first_subscribed_at` the start of the very first.
